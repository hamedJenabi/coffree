# MokkaClub — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Product Summary

**MokkaClub** is a paid membership for people who frequently buy coffee, matcha, tea, and similar drinks at independent cafés.

The initial launch market is **Vienna**.

The core consumer proposition is:

**Pay a monthly membership fee and receive discounts and member benefits at participating cafés.**

Initial founding offer:

- approximately €4.99/month
- approximately 20% off selected drinks or café-controlled offers
- independent cafés
- Vienna-first
- mobile-first web experience

The original concept was inspired by a similar coffee membership experienced by a friend in London. The underlying observation is that Vienna has a strong café culture and a large population of people who spend repeatedly at independent coffee shops.

MokkaClub should initially be treated as a **business hypothesis to validate**, not as a software project that needs months of development.

# 2. Current Name

Working brand:

# **MokkaClub**

Previous names considered:

- Sip Club — rejected because a very similar concept already operates under that name elsewhere
- MokkaCup — considered, but “Cup” sounds more like a physical coffee product than a membership

MokkaClub is currently preferred because:

- “Club” immediately communicates membership
- “Mokka” is strongly associated with coffee in the DACH region
- it has a more Vienna/Austrian character than a generic English coffee name
- it can support a community/lifestyle identity
- it sounds natural in phrases such as “MokkaClub member”

Potential positioning:

> **MokkaClub — One membership. Vienna's best cafés.**

or:

> **MokkaClub — Your café membership for Vienna.**

Before major branding investment, the name must still receive proper:

- trademark checks
- company-name checks
- domain checks
- social-handle checks
- app-store checks

The name should not yet be considered legally cleared.

## Cafés

# 9. Core Consumer Problem

Frequent café customers can spend substantial amounts each month on drinks.

Currently, a user often has:

- individual loyalty cards
- scattered café apps
- no unified membership across venues
- no meaningful discount network focused specifically on cafés
- little incentive to discover independent venues through one membership

MokkaClub offers:

**one membership across multiple cafés.**

---

# 10. Core Café Problem

Cafés need customers, but they do not necessarily want blanket discounts.

A key risk is:

### Cannibalization

If someone already visits Café A four times per week and suddenly receives 20% off through MokkaClub, the café may simply lose margin.

Therefore, MokkaClub should **not** primarily position itself as:

> “Give your regular customers cheaper drinks.”

Instead:

> **“MokkaClub helps you bring in customers who otherwise would not have visited.”**

The café proposition should focus on:

- incremental visits
- new customers
- slow-hour utilization
- measurable customer acquisition
- repeat behavior

# 12. Café-Controlled Offers

A blanket 20% discount everywhere may not be the optimal long-term system.

Cafés should eventually control:

- eligible products
- eligible days
- eligible hours
- discount percentages
- redemption limits
- temporary campaigns

Example:

**Monday–Friday, 14:00–17:00  
20% off matcha and iced drinks**

This converts:

**unused capacity → incremental revenue**

rather than discounting all existing transactions.

---

# 13. Redemption System

Avoid POS integrations during the pilot.

Initial flow:

1. Customer orders.
2. Customer opens MokkaClub.
3. Customer displays a dynamic QR or short code.
4. Café verifies membership.
5. Café applies the discount manually.
6. MokkaClub records the redemption.

Potential recorded data:

- member_id
- cafe_id
- timestamp
- offer_id
- original price
- discount
- final transaction amount
- category/product type

The long-term strategic value comes from the resulting transaction data.

# 22. Defensibility

The software itself is not the moat.

Potential defensibility comes from:

## Network effects

More cafés make membership more useful.

More members make MokkaClub more valuable to cafés.

## Venue relationships

Strong partnerships with desirable cafés can be difficult to reproduce.

## Local density

A competitor with 50 cafés across a city may be weaker than MokkaClub with 25 excellent cafés concentrated in one neighborhood.

## Brand

MokkaClub could become culturally associated with café discovery and independent coffee culture.

## Data

Over time, MokkaClub could understand:

- visit frequency
- customer movement
- offer elasticity
- time-of-day demand
- café conversion
- retention
- customer preferences
- neighborhood demand

## B2B integration

If cafés use MokkaClub for acquisition and analytics, switching costs increase.

## Employer distribution

MokkaClub for Work could create another defensible customer acquisition channel.

# 23. Main Risks

## 1. Café cannibalization

Existing customers receive discounts on purchases they would have made anyway.

Mitigation:

- time-limited offers
- product-specific offers
- café-controlled offers
- new-customer promotions
- measurement of incremental visits

## 2. Marketplace chicken-and-egg problem

Consumers want cafés.

Cafés want consumers.

Mitigation:

Concentrate aggressively in one neighborhood and manually seed both sides.

## 3. Low willingness to pay

Consumers may like the concept but refuse to subscribe.

Mitigation:

Collect actual payment as early as possible.

## 4. Weak consumer economics

€4.99 subscriptions alone may not support a very large company.

Mitigation:

- higher-value memberships
- B2B SaaS
- transaction revenue
- corporate benefits
- city expansion

## 5. Copyability

A competitor could recreate the basic app.

Mitigation:

- relationships
- brand
- local density
- data
- B2B tools
- expansion speed

## 6. Discount-brand perception

Consumers may perceive MokkaClub as a cheap coupon app.

Mitigation:

Focus on curation, community and member benefits rather than permanent blanket discounts.

# 29. Kill / Continue Logic

MokkaClub should be treated as an experiment.

Do not spend six months rationalizing weak demand.

Possible strong continue signals:

- approximately 10 cafés commit relatively quickly
- strangers pay for membership
- members use the product repeatedly
- cafés see useful traffic
- cafés want to continue after the pilot
- users refer friends
- customer acquisition cost appears manageable

Possible warning signals:

- cafés only join when pushed heavily
- customers like the concept but refuse to pay
- users subscribe but rarely redeem
- users cancel after one month
- cafés complain about margin loss
- most redemptions are from existing regulars
- expansion requires excessive manual effort

# 30. Legal / Company Structure

MokkaClub should **not initially be treated as a large formal startup merely for appearances**.

First validate the business.

There are two separate concepts:

## Startup

This describes the business ambition:

a scalable company searching for a repeatable business model.

## Legal company structure

This describes how the business is legally operated.

Examples in Austria include:

- Einzelunternehmen
- GmbH
- FlexCo

The current strategy:

### Validation phase

Keep the setup simple and inexpensive.

Before regularly accepting commercial revenue or signing meaningful agreements, obtain appropriate Austrian legal/tax guidance and establish whatever registration is required.

### After meaningful validation

Consider a more formal limited-liability company structure once:

- recurring revenue becomes meaningful
- café contracts become significant
- liability increases
- a cofounder joins
- employees are hired
- investors become relevant
- equity needs to be issued

A **FlexCo** may deserve particular consideration if MokkaClub develops into a multi-founder or venture-backed startup.

A **GmbH** may also be appropriate depending on ownership and financing strategy.

The exact legal/tax structure should be decided with Austrian professional advice once commercial validation exists.
